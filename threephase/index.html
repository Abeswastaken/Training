How to

The power circuit (top) follows the example you load. Below it is the control ladder: X1 on the left, X2 on the right. Tap an empty slot on a rung to pick a part (with search), or drag one from this list. Contacts and switches go on the left, one load (coil, motor, light) on the right. Tick "parallel branch" in the slot menu, or drop a part in a dashed "branch" box, to put it in parallel. Drag a placed part to move it (long-press on a phone). + and − beside a rung add or remove slots. Tap a placed part to see it, check it, or remove it.

Try it

Swap two leads and the motor runs backward. Lose a phase while it runs and it keeps turning on two lines, drawing about 1.73 times normal current until the overload trips; start it single phased and it just hums.

Code and practice notes

  • Full-load amps come from NEC Table 430.250 (10 hp: 30.8 A at 208 V, 28 A at 230 V, 14 A at 460 V). Size conductors and short-circuit protection from the table, overloads from the nameplate.
  • Overloads (430.32): 125% of nameplate FLA for motors with a service factor of 1.15 or more or a temperature rise of 40°C or less; 115% for others.
  • The fuses here are the branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection (430.52); they don't protect the motor from overload.
  • The control transformer is protected on its secondary (430.72). X2 is grounded. Coils go on the X2 side (overload contacts are the usual exception allowed between a coil and X2), and switches and contacts sit on X1, so a ground fault blows the fuse instead of starting the motor.
  • Disconnect in sight of the motor and controller (430.102).
  • Wire colors: black/red/blue on 208/240 V and brown/orange/yellow on 480 V are common practice, not code. The NEC only requires the high leg of a 4-wire delta to be orange (110.15), and sets white/gray for the grounded conductor and green for equipment grounds.

Training aid only

3-Phase Motor Control is a simplified simulator for learning motor starter power and control circuits. It is not a design tool or a substitute for training, the equipment's wiring diagram, or the electrical code.

  • Each rung is checked as logic: closed contacts pass power, and a rung with no load is treated as a dead short.
  • Real arc flash and shock can kill. De-energize, lock out, tag out, and verify dead with a tested meter before you touch wiring.
